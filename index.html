01 · About

How I got here.

I started building computers in high school, which is where the questions began: what a CPU is actually doing, and why a GPU is so much faster at some things than others.

In 2024 I trained my first model at Carnegie Mellon's AI Scholars program, then came to Stanford through the QuestBridge Match as a Gates Scholar. Since then I have worked at most layers of the stack: modelling collision risk in multi-robot systems at the Multi-Robotic Systems Lab, building ShotSpot at TreeHacks, writing CUDA kernels, and building circuits from components.

This summer I am on AWS EC2 Gateway Networking, building tooling that flags failing nodes across 30+ regions and an agent that triages on-call tickets before an engineer opens them.

04 · Hardware

Things that run on a breadboard.

Physical builds - circuits, boards and embedded systems. These are not on GitHub, so each one is written up here in full: what it does, what is inside it, what was tricky, and what I took away.

  • May 2026

    Electrocardiogram

    An analog front end that pulls a heartbeat out of the noise. The signal at the skin is a few millivolts sitting under interference many times larger, so the circuit amplifies by 600× while throwing away everything that isn't a heartbeat.

    Read the build

  • May 2026

    8×8 LED Music Visualizer

    Sixty-four LEDs driven from sixteen pins by scanning the grid faster than the eye can follow - then wired to an audio input so the display reacts to music in real time, running an FFT on an 8-bit microcontroller.

    Read the build

  • May 2026

    Useless Box

    A box whose only function is to switch itself off. Flip the toggle and an arm drives out, flips it back, and retreats - a microcontroller and an H-bridge doing something completely pointless, on purpose.

05 · Next

Systems for AI.

Large-scale training is the one problem where every layer I have worked on matters at once. Each GPU computes a noisy estimate of the gradient, all-reduce averages them across the cluster, and the hard part is doing that across thousands of accelerators without the network becoming the bottleneck. That is where my networking work and my ML work meet.

The other direction interests me just as much: models that operate infrastructure. The on-call triage agent and the LLM scheduling advisor were first steps toward systems that diagnose and tune themselves.
